How is your net pay calculated?

1. Before taxes: your traditional 401(k)/403(b) lowers income tax; your health, dental and vision insurance, FSA and HSA through payroll also lower Social Security and Medicare.

2. Federal income tax: annual pay − pre-tax deductions − standard (or itemized) deduction, through the IRS progressive table for your filing status; minus child and dependent credits. Spread over 12 months.

3. Social Security and Medicare (FICA): 6.2% up to the annual wage base and 1.45% (plus 0.9% over $200,000).

4. State and city: Michigan charges a flat rate after a per-person exemption; some cities (Detroit, Grand Rapids…) have their own income tax. Other states: type the rate from your pay stub.

5. Net: pay − FICA − taxes − your other paycheck deductions. It's an estimate: check it against your pay stub and with a tax professional.

How is your net pay calculated?

1. Social security contribution: Gross salary × personal contribution rate (9.45% by default).

2. Taxable income: annual salary − annual IESS, run through SRI's progressive table.

3. Personal-expense rebate: 18% of your actual personal expenses (housing, health, education, food, clothing, tourism) is taken off the tax from the table, up to a cap of 7 basic baskets with no dependents and up to 20 with five or more. The remaining tax is withheld in 12 installments.

4. Net: Salary − social security − monthly withholding. In December and in your region's month the 13th and 14th-month bonuses are added (no social security on them).

    Snowball or Avalanche? Where does the money come from?

    Debt Snowball (recommended by Dave Ramsey): pay the SMALLEST balance first while making the minimums on the rest. When a debt is gone, its payment is added to the next one. Quick wins keep you motivated.

    Avalanche: pay the highest RATE first. It saves more total interest, but the first wins take longer.

    The money: the plan uses everything your base budget for assigns to "Debt payments". It first covers each debt's minimum; whatever's left goes entirely to the debt that's next in the strategy. When a debt is paid off, its money moves to the next one. To finish sooner, raise a debt's amount here or in the budget (and lower another line so it fits).

    How is the time calculated? (NPER)

    Months = ln[(Goal × r + Contribution) / (Saved × r + Contribution)] / ln(1 + r), with r = annual rate ÷ 12. Money already saved earns interest too, so the real time is shorter than dividing what's left by the contribution.

    How is compound interest on a CD calculated?

    At maturity (simple): Interest = Principal × Rate × (Days / 360), paid at the end of the term.

    Compound (monthly, quarterly, semiannual, annual): Interest = Principal × [(1 + Rate/n)^(n × Days/360) − 1], with n = 12, 4, 2 or 1. The more often it compounds, the more it grows.

    Projection: each year the accumulated balance earns interest; new savings arrive through the year, so they earn half a year of interest. Starting early matters more than contributing a lot.

    How are the French and German systems calculated?

    French (fixed payment): Payment = Principal × i / (1 − (1+i)⁻ⁿ), where i is the monthly rate and n the months. Interest goes down and principal goes up, but the payment stays the same. It's how almost every fixed-rate loan works.

    German (declining payment): Fixed principal payment = Principal / n; interest is charged on the balance, so the payment starts high and goes down. Less total interest, but more is needed at the start.

    Social Security estimate: uses your current pay as the 35-year average (with the annual cap), the benefit formula (90% / 32% / 15%) and your claiming age (less before 67, more up to 70). Your real estimate is at ssa.gov/myaccount.

    The real IESS formula is more complex (contribution history, minimum years, reforms). Here the pension is reduced proportionally if your total career is under 30 years. Check your case at iess.gob.ec.

    How is it calculated?

    Future value: FV = Savings × (1+i)ⁿ + Contribution × [((1+i)ⁿ − 1) / i], with i the monthly rate and n the months until retirement.

    Income from savings: (FV × safe withdrawal rate) / 12.

    Social Security: Average salary × replacement rate × min(1, total years / 30).

    Social Security: monthly average of your 35 best years (with the annual cap) → 90% of the first $1,286 + 32% up to $7,749 + 15% of the rest; less if you claim before 67, 8% more per year up to 70.

    Why does net worth matter more than income? How does selling an asset work?

    Your income is a flow; your net worth (Assets − Liabilities) is a snapshot of your real wealth. What matters is the trend: that it grows year after year as you pay off debt and build savings.

    Value by year: the "Value in [year]" applies to the active year and the following ones until you change it again (appreciation or depreciation).

    Sell: from the year it's sold the asset stops counting. You can move the sale money to that year's "Checking accounts" with one click, so your net worth doesn't jump: it just goes from a physical asset to cash.
